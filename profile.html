Profile

Current Position

Postdoctoral researcher (2024–present) at the Center for Landscape Research in Sustainable Agricultural Futures (Land-CRAFT) and the Department of Agroecology, Aarhus University, Denmark.

Farm- and landscape-scale nitrogen modelling using process-based models and data-driven approaches.

Scientific Identity

I am a nitrogen systems scientist who bridges field observations with global assessments to expose overlooked nitrogen inputs and develop policy-relevant indicators. My work focuses on nitrogen use efficiency, environmental losses, and the science-policy interface in agricultural systems. I contribute to nitrogen governance initiatives through Land-CRAFT, EUNEP, and FAO collaborations to translate scientific evidence into actionable policy solutions.

Scientific Roles & Leadership

I serve as a member of the EU Nitrogen Expert Panel (Crop Working Group), contributing to European nitrogen policy development and assessment. As a team member of the FAO Cropland Nutrient Balance Team, I support global nutrient accounting and sustainable agriculture initiatives. I hold lead authorship responsibilities in the International Nitrogen Assessment, coordinating synthesis of scientific evidence on nitrogen cycling and environmental impacts across agricultural systems.

Software Development

R environment

  • R package rCTOOL that provides a carbon turnover model for cropland soils (joint effort with Dr. Franca Gianni-Kurina, Aarhus University).
  • R package emepManure that provides an inventory-based approach to estimate NH3, N2O and NOx emissions from manure management systems.
  • R package livCH4ipcc that provides an inventory-based approach to estimate CH4 emissions from enteric fermentation and manure management systems.

Python environment

  • Development of an internal tool pyLDNDC to configure and run Landscape-DNDC.
  • Refactoring of internal AU model FarmAC to Python package pyFarmAC.
  • Development of FAO Global water balance model and formalization into Python package pyglobwb.

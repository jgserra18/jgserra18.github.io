Scientific Findings

Key Research Contributions

Irrigation water is a systematic and overlooked nitrogen input in agriculture.123
Through field-based evidence and large-scale assessments, I demonstrated that nitrogen supplied via irrigation water constitutes a non-negligible and spatially heterogeneous input to cropland systems. Ignoring this input leads to biased nitrogen balances, distorted estimates of nitrogen use efficiency, and misinformed fertilisation policies, particularly in irrigated and groundwater-dependent regions.

Current nitrate vulnerable zones in Europe fail to capture major groundwater pollution hotspots.4
By combining groundwater vulnerability, nitrogen inputs, and observed nitrate concentrations, I identified widespread mismatches between designated Nitrate Vulnerable Zones and actual groundwater contamination risk. This reveals structural weaknesses in current regulatory approaches and highlights the need for evidence-based spatial targeting.

Timelapse of groundwater nitrate hotspots in Europe (1992-2019). Concentrations above 50 mg/L denote hotspots that should be within Nitrate Vulnerable Zones.

Large areas of cropland operate outside agronomically viable and environmentally safe nitrogen boundaries.5
My work shows that inefficient or unsustainable nitrogen performance are not solely the result of poor management at the margin, but often reflect structural constraints related to crop composition, yield ceilings, and regional production systems. In these contexts, further efficiency gains are limited unless structural drivers are explicitly addressed. My results point to how performance is often locked-in and difficult to improve without substantial transformations in domestic food production-consumption systems.
  • Constrained space: Nitrogen inputs are insufficient to meet crop demand, resulting in yield gaps, soil nutrient depletion, and risks to food security.
  • Safe space: Nitrogen inputs support crop productivity while remaining within agronomically and environmentally acceptable limits.
  • Wasteful space: Nitrogen inputs exceed crop demand, leading to low nitrogen use efficiency and elevated losses to air and water.
  • Unsustainable space: Structural constraints, such as crop composition and yield ceilings, prevent further efficiency gains and trap systems outside viable long-term nitrogen use.

Science-Policy Interface in Nitrogen Governance6

My work bridges the gap between scientific evidence and policy implementation, identifying barriers and solutions to effective nitrogen management across different governance levels and geographical contexts.

By relating global research output to cropland nitrogen surplus, I identified a persistent mismatch between nitrogen pollution severity and scientific effort across regions.

Methodological Innovations

Integration of Multi-Scale Approaches
Successfully combined field observations, process-based biogeochemical models, and machine-learning techniques to upscale evidence and reduce uncertainty in nitrogen assessments across spatial scales.
Development of Policy-Relevant Indicators
Created agronomically meaningful and policy-relevant nitrogen indicators that support both scientific understanding and practical decision-making in sustainable agriculture.
